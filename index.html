FloorPlanz a breaking set dictionary · prototype, built on BRACE

Read this first. This whole thing is vibe coded: I sketched it with an AI coding assistant over a couple of days. Use it with caution. Nothing really works properly yet. Recognition is guesswork, things will break, and your data only lives in this browser. It's a proof of concept, a way of showing what a breaking set dictionary could be, not a finished tool.

What this is

FloorPlanz is a first sketch of a breaking set dictionary and calculator. The idea: give it footage and it logs your set. Which moves, in what order, how they chain, who did them first. For now you do most of the naming by hand, and the app helps you see, connect and remember the moves.

It's Part 1 of my Dreams in Draft series: pipe dreams I'm putting on the internet so they exist somewhere outside my head.

How to use it

  1. 1 · Watch & name. Browse Red Bull BC One rounds with a skeleton drawn over the dancer, or 3D motion-capture clips you can rotate. Use I / O to set In and Out around a move, then pick its name from the dropdown (139 moves; typing a nickname snaps to the main name). By move groups everything into Toprock / Footwork / Power / Freeze, including moves from your own sessions. Detected freezes are 103 moments where the body goes still; none of them have names yet.
  2. 2 · Connect. Click moves to build a chain. Each arrow tells you whether that transition is in a set you can already do, was seen back to back in a BC One round, or has never been connected. Where next suggests transitions you haven't tried. One in the chamber gives you two random moves to join (an idea from The Break).
  3. 3 · Sets & ideas. The gym-app side. Log sets you can do, and ideas you can't try right now (injured, no floor, on a train). Tick "I did it" when you land one. Combos you save from your sessions show up here too.
  4. 4 · My sessions. Your own footage. Pick a clip, or add your own (they stay in this browser). Press Track my body only to put a skeleton on it. Set In/Out around each move and name it, the same as in tab 1. Save the whole sequence as a combo, then Recognise moves to see whether it can spot your moves and combos in other clips. Rename sessions and clips with ✎. Custom names are fine: not every move has a name, and not every combo is strict. I label toprock and footwork loosely ("random top rock combo", "6 step 3 step combo") because that's where breaking is most free, and only power moves and freezes get their own exact names.
  5. Back up. Press Export JSON now and then. Clearing your browser data wipes everything.

What works, what doesn't

  • Works-ish: naming and connecting moves, combos, sessions, body tracking on clear single-dancer footage.
  • Barely: recognition. It compares body shapes against moves you've labelled yourself. On the clip it learned from it scores 8/8, which proves nothing because it's marking its own homework. On two clips it had never seen, it found nothing on one and called footwork a chair freeze on the other.
  • Breaks: upside-down and spinning bodies (the tracker was trained on people standing up), several people in frame, dark footage, long clips, phones.
  • Not built: flavour, personal style, "who did it first", timing on the beat, live battle analytics.

Where the data comes from

BRACE: the Breakdancing Competition Dataset
Davide Moltisanti, Jinyi Wu, Bo Dai & Chen Change Loy. ECCV 2022. NTU S-Lab, Singapore, with Shanghai AI Lab. A 2D skeleton on every frame of Red Bull BC One battles, with segments labelled toprock / footwork / powermove. That's where the skeletons in tab 1 come from. Paper · Data. Licence: S-Lab License 1.0, non-commercial only. I fixed the frame rates per video (57 at 25 fps, 23 at 30 fps) and relabelled clips "who vs who", because the dancer tags were often wrong. The freeze detection is mine, not BRACE's.
Red Bull BC One footage
The video under the skeletons. It's embedded from Red Bull's own YouTube uploads, never downloaded or copied. Red Bull owns it, and the dancers in it never agreed to be training data (see Ethics below).
CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database
Carnegie Mellon University, mocap.cs.cmu.edu. Subject 85 ("jumps; flips; breakdance") and trial 90_28: 3D marker motion capture. I kept CMU's original trial names and added my own classification, because the names are often off. "90TwistsFall" is actually four swipes and a fall, for example.
FraGue's Breakdance Move List
FraGue Moser-Kindler, frague.at. 136 moves with nicknames, who taught them and, for a few, who created them (Roxrite's Corkstep, Penny's 6 Step, Pepito's Sweep, Crumbs' Starwalk). It's the backbone of the naming dropdown.
Wikipedia
Short definitions for 13 moves (CC BY-SA).
MediaPipe Pose Landmarker
Google (Apache 2.0). Tracks the body in your own clips, running entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
Your own clips
The three demo clips in "Session · 20 Oct 2026" are my own training footage.

Research I read

Why computers can't see breaking yet
Needham, Evans, Cosker & Colyer 2021, Sensors: markerless pose tracking does well on sprinting and fails on unusual body positions, because the models only learned from everyday upright photos.
Labuguen, Ingco, Kogami, Blanco Negrete & Shibata 2020: on pop dance, markerless tracking is close to marker mocap on average, but fails when limbs hide each other.
Desmarais, Mottet, Slangen & Montesinos 2021: a review of 3D pose methods. The lab benchmarks they're tested on don't have headspins in them.
Breaking as sport and data
Lerebourg & Guignard 2024: models performance in breaking and argues it needs notation and video coding. Its toprock / footwork / power / freeze split shapes the columns here.
Dhar, Ramakrishnan & Munson 2025: video AI on BRACE's three labels. Best about 69% of frames right. Named moves not attempted.
Sato et al. 2025 and Braeunig 2025: Paris 2024 Olympic judging. Judges agreed most on originality, and there's evidence of home-nation bias.
Archives and movement
Sarah Whatley 2017, on digital dance archives and what gets recorded. Étienne-Jules Marey's chronophotographs inspired the frame-by-frame view in tab 1.
Datasets that don't quite fit (yet)
AIST++ (breaking is about 10% of it, mostly simple upright moves), Motorica Dance Dataset (hip hop, popping, locking, krump; no breaking), DanceFlow / DanceCrafter (Yuan et al. 2026; has a breaking genre with long text descriptions, not move names, and its code isn't out yet), FineGym (gymnastics with 99 named elements, which is the model for what a breaking version could look like).

People already doing pieces of this

  • MovesBook (movesbook.app) by Beppe Monteleone, bboy Thai-Rock: the closest thing to this. A training journal, move library, lineage tree and sets with an energy curve. No AI, on purpose. I'd rather work with them than compete.
  • The Break (thebreak.app) by Scott Tolinski: move book, battle mode, and "One in the chamber". The gym-app side, already built.
  • Breakbitebase (@breakbitebase on Instagram): side-by-side clips, and the comments decide "bite or base". The closest thing to a "who did it first" archive, and it's community-run.
  • Tricking: Loopkicks Tricktionary, TrickAtlas, Trickipedia. BJJ: GrappleMap. Each is a move map in its own discipline.
  • Inspiration: Floor Kids (MERJ Media) for the love of breaking movement drawn frame by frame. Wandance (Coffee) for showing dance on the page. And my own childhood wish for a dance dictionary.

Ethics: the part I'm unsure about

Motion data for breaking excites me. It could change how tech understands bodies. But the easy way to make money from this is selling the data, and I don't want big tech profiting off my friends. Even BRACE, the data this is built on, uses breakers who never agreed to be training data. If this ever becomes real, it's consent first: dancers opt in, the community holds the data, and money flows back. I'm looking at Te Hiku Media's Kaitiakitanga licence as one model.

I also don't want AI to be the one doing this. The prototype was sketched with AI, but a real version needs b-boys and b-girls who care about the history, building it together. It shouldn't become a points game or an automated judge.

Where it could go

  1. Recognise combos you've labelled before, in new footage.
  2. Spot variations: a step cut, a move added, the order swapped.
  3. Timing: you slowed a move down on the beat, held a freeze longer.
  4. Flavour: your six-step versus a standard one. Your personal style.
  5. Name every move specifically, even inside free-flowing toprock and footwork, once enough breakers have labelled enough clips. That needs a community.
  6. The big one: a dictionary that remembers who did it first, and live battle analytics like Hawk-Eye in tennis or MLB Gameday.

Tech notes, for the curious

  • One HTML page plus a few scripts. No server, no account, no tracking. Everything you name is saved in your browser's local storage, and uploaded videos in IndexedDB.
  • Body tracking: MediaPipe in the browser, sampled while the clip plays. With several people in frame it follows whoever is closest to the first body it found.
  • Move suggestions: the clip is split where the body changes most (dynamic programming over joint motion).
  • Recognition: each frame becomes a body-shape fingerprint (13 joints relative to the hips, scaled by torso length, mirrored too) and is matched against the moves you've labelled. Combos are matched by move order, allowing gaps.
  • Freeze detection on BRACE: the body almost still (joints moving under 3.5% of torso length) for at least 0.45s while low or upside down.
